# Internal Pressure Simulation for a Steam Drum with an Exit Steam Valve

## 1. Purpose and Physical Picture

A steam drum is a pressurized vessel that contains a two-phase mixture of liquid water and steam. The internal pressure changes because of the competition between:

- heat added to the water/steam inventory,
- steam generated by boiling,
- steam removed through the exit valve,
- feedwater or condensate inflow, if present,
- liquid blowdown or other drains, if present,
- changes in the liquid and vapor volumes inside the fixed tank.

The exit steam valve is important because it converts pressure difference into mass flow. When the valve opens, steam inventory leaves the drum. If steam leaves faster than it is generated, pressure falls. If heat input generates steam faster than the valve removes it, pressure rises.

This document describes the thermodynamics and equations needed to simulate the internal drum pressure.

## 2. Main Assumptions

A practical first model usually assumes:

- The drum is a fixed-volume control volume.
- The liquid and vapor phases are well mixed.
- The liquid and vapor are in thermodynamic equilibrium at the drum pressure.
- The drum contents are saturated, so temperature is determined by pressure.
- Steam exiting the top of the drum is saturated vapor, or slightly superheated if a superheat model is added.
- Kinetic and potential energy inside the drum are negligible compared with internal energy and enthalpy.
- The wall heat capacity may be ignored in a simple model, or added as a separate energy-storage term.

These assumptions lead to a lumped dynamic model. More advanced models can relax the equilibrium assumption and use separate liquid and vapor temperatures.

## 3. Important State Variables

For a fixed-volume steam drum:

| Symbol | Meaning | Units |
|---|---|---|
| `P` | drum pressure | Pa |
| `T` | drum temperature | K |
| `V` | total internal drum volume | m3 |
| `V_l` | liquid volume | m3 |
| `V_v` | vapor volume | m3 |
| `m_l` | liquid water mass | kg |
| `m_v` | steam mass | kg |
| `m` | total mass, `m_l + m_v` | kg |
| `x` | vapor quality, `m_v / m` | - |
| `u_l` | saturated-liquid specific internal energy | J/kg |
| `u_v` | saturated-vapor specific internal energy | J/kg |
| `h_l` | saturated-liquid specific enthalpy | J/kg |
| `h_v` | saturated-vapor specific enthalpy | J/kg |
| `rho_l` | saturated-liquid density | kg/m3 |
| `rho_v` | saturated-vapor density | kg/m3 |
| `Q_dot` | heat-transfer rate into the drum | W |
| `m_dot_out` | steam mass flow through exit valve | kg/s |
| `m_dot_in` | feedwater mass flow into drum | kg/s |

For saturated equilibrium:

```text
T = T_sat(P)
u_l = u_f(P)
u_v = u_g(P)
h_l = h_f(P)
h_v = h_g(P)
rho_l = rho_f(P)
rho_v = rho_g(P)
```

The subscripts `f` and `g` are common steam-table notation:

- `f`: saturated liquid,
- `g`: saturated vapor.

## 4. Saturation Constraint

If the drum contains both liquid and vapor in equilibrium, pressure and temperature are not independent:

```text
T = T_sat(P)
P = P_sat(T)
```

The water inventory must also satisfy the fixed-volume constraint:

```text
V = V_l + V_v
V = m_l / rho_l(P) + m_v / rho_v(P)
```

Using total mass `m` and quality `x`:

```text
m_l = (1 - x)m
m_v = x m
```

The specific volume of the mixture is:

```text
v = V / m
v = (1 - x)v_f(P) + x v_g(P)
```

where:

```text
v_f = 1 / rho_l
v_g = 1 / rho_v
```

The quality can be recovered from pressure and total mass:

```text
x = (V/m - v_f(P)) / (v_g(P) - v_f(P))
```

This relation is valid only when `0 <= x <= 1`. If `x < 0`, the drum is compressed liquid only. If `x > 1`, the drum is vapor only or superheated steam.

## 5. Mass Balance

The total mass balance for the drum is:

```text
dm/dt = sum(m_dot_in) - sum(m_dot_out)
```

For one feedwater inlet and one exit steam valve:

```text
dm/dt = m_dot_fw - m_dot_s
```

where:

- `m_dot_fw` is feedwater entering the drum,
- `m_dot_s` is steam leaving through the valve.

If there is blowdown:

```text
dm/dt = m_dot_fw - m_dot_s - m_dot_bd
```

For a simple steam-only discharge valve, the outgoing flow is usually taken from the vapor space:

```text
h_out = h_g(P)
```

If liquid carryover exists, the outgoing enthalpy should include outlet quality:

```text
h_out = (1 - x_out)h_f(P) + x_out h_g(P)
```

## 6. Energy Balance

For a fixed control volume with negligible kinetic and potential energy:

```text
dU/dt = Q_dot - W_dot + sum(m_dot_in h_in) - sum(m_dot_out h_out)
```

For a rigid tank, boundary work is zero:

```text
W_dot = 0
```

So:

```text
dU/dt = Q_dot + m_dot_fw h_fw - m_dot_s h_s - m_dot_bd h_bd
```

For saturated steam leaving the top:

```text
h_s = h_g(P)
```

For saturated-liquid blowdown:

```text
h_bd = h_f(P)
```

The internal energy stored inside the two-phase drum is:

```text
U = m_l u_f(P) + m_v u_g(P)
```

or:

```text
U = m[(1 - x)u_f(P) + x u_g(P)]
```

Define mixture internal energy:

```text
u = (1 - x)u_f(P) + x u_g(P)
```

Then:

```text
U = m u
```

## 7. Pressure as the Dynamic State

To simulate pressure, one convenient state choice is:

```text
states: m, U
```

At each time step, solve the thermodynamic state from:

```text
m = m_l + m_v
U = m_l u_f(P) + m_v u_g(P)
V = m_l v_f(P) + m_v v_g(P)
```

The unknowns are usually `P`, `m_l`, and `m_v`. Once `P` is found, pressure is known directly.

This state choice is robust because the differential equations are simple:

```text
dm/dt = m_dot_fw - m_dot_s - m_dot_bd
dU/dt = Q_dot + m_dot_fw h_fw - m_dot_s h_g(P) - m_dot_bd h_f(P)
```

The pressure is then computed algebraically from `m`, `U`, and `V`.

## 8. Alternative Pressure Derivative Form

Sometimes it is useful to write an explicit pressure derivative. For a saturated two-phase equilibrium system:

```text
U(P, m) = m[(1 - x)u_f(P) + x u_g(P)]
```

with:

```text
x(P, m) = (V/m - v_f(P)) / (v_g(P) - v_f(P))
```

Because `U` depends on both `P` and `m`:

```text
dU/dt = (partial U / partial P)_m dP/dt
      + (partial U / partial m)_P dm/dt
```

Therefore:

```text
dP/dt = [dU/dt - (partial U / partial m)_P dm/dt]
        / (partial U / partial P)_m
```

This is useful for ordinary differential equation solvers that use `P` and `m` as the states. However, it requires accurate derivatives of steam-table properties with respect to pressure. Numerically, the derivatives can be computed using finite differences or a thermodynamic property library.

## 9. Valve Mass Flow Model

The exit valve connects the drum pressure `P` to a downstream pressure `P_down`. The mass flow depends on:

- upstream drum pressure,
- downstream pressure,
- steam temperature and density,
- valve opening,
- valve coefficient,
- whether the flow is choked.

### 9.1 Simple Incompressible-Like Approximation

For small pressure drops, a simple approximation is:

```text
m_dot_s = C_v_eff sqrt(rho_v(P) max(P - P_down, 0))
```

or:

```text
m_dot_s = C_d A_valve sqrt(2 rho_v(P) max(P - P_down, 0))
```

where:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `C_d` | discharge coefficient |
| `A_valve` | effective open area |
| `C_v_eff` | effective valve flow coefficient |
| `P_down` | downstream pressure |

This approximation is easy to use but is less accurate for large steam pressure drops because steam is compressible.

### 9.2 Compressible Gas Valve Flow

For steam treated approximately as an ideal gas at the valve inlet:

```text
rho = P / (R_s T)
```

where `R_s` is the specific gas constant for water vapor:

```text
R_s = 461.5 J/(kg K)
```

Let:

```text
r = P_down / P
gamma = c_p / c_v
```

For subcritical, non-choked flow:

```text
m_dot_s = C_d A P sqrt[
  (2 gamma / (R_s T (gamma - 1)))
  (r^(2/gamma) - r^((gamma + 1)/gamma))
]
```

For choked flow:

```text
m_dot_s = C_d A P sqrt[
  gamma / (R_s T)
  (2 / (gamma + 1))^((gamma + 1)/(gamma - 1))
]
```

Choking occurs when:

```text
r <= r_crit
```

with:

```text
r_crit = (2 / (gamma + 1))^(gamma / (gamma - 1))
```

For steam, `gamma` is often around `1.25` to `1.33`, depending on pressure and temperature. For accurate work, use real-steam properties rather than a fixed ideal-gas value.

### 9.3 Valve Opening

The effective area can be related to valve opening `alpha`:

```text
A = A_max f(alpha)
```

where:

```text
0 <= alpha <= 1
```

Common choices:

Linear valve:

```text
f(alpha) = alpha
```

Equal-percentage valve:

```text
f(alpha) = (R_v^alpha - 1) / (R_v - 1)
```

where `R_v` is the valve rangeability.

## 10. Heat Input and Steam Generation

The heat input `Q_dot` may come from burners, electric heaters, process heat, or a boiler tube bundle. In the lumped drum model, heat input increases internal energy:

```text
dU/dt includes Q_dot
```

If the drum is saturated, additional energy mainly causes evaporation rather than a large temperature increase. The approximate evaporation rate due to heat input alone is:

```text
m_dot_evap approx Q_dot / h_fg
```

where:

```text
h_fg = h_g(P) - h_f(P)
```

This expression is useful for intuition, but the full model should use the total mass and energy balances because pressure, quality, and stored energy change at the same time.

## 11. Feedwater Effects

Feedwater changes both mass and energy:

```text
dm/dt includes +m_dot_fw
dU/dt includes +m_dot_fw h_fw
```

If feedwater is subcooled liquid:

```text
h_fw approx h_liquid(T_fw, P_fw)
```

If detailed liquid properties are unavailable, an engineering approximation is:

```text
h_fw approx c_p,w (T_fw - T_ref)
```

relative to the same enthalpy reference used in the steam tables.

Cold feedwater can reduce pressure because it adds mass but absorbs energy to reach saturation.

## 12. Wall Heat Capacity

For heavy steel vessels, the drum wall can store significant energy. A simple wall model is:

```text
m_wall c_wall dT_wall/dt = Q_dot_external - Q_dot_to_fluid - Q_dot_loss
```

Heat transferred from wall to fluid:

```text
Q_dot_to_fluid = U_A (T_wall - T_fluid)
```

Then the fluid energy balance becomes:

```text
dU_fluid/dt = Q_dot_to_fluid + m_dot_fw h_fw - m_dot_s h_s - m_dot_bd h_bd
```

This improves transient predictions during startup, shutdown, and fast load changes.

## 13. Heat Losses

Heat loss to the environment can be modeled as:

```text
Q_dot_loss = U_loss A_ext (T_drum - T_amb)
```

or with radiation:

```text
Q_dot_rad = epsilon sigma A_ext (T_wall^4 - T_amb^4)
```

Net heat into the fluid:

```text
Q_dot_net = Q_dot_added - Q_dot_loss
```

Use `Q_dot_net` in the energy balance.

## 14. Complete Minimal Dynamic Model

A useful minimal model is:

```text
known:
  V
  Q_dot(t)
  m_dot_fw(t)
  h_fw(t)
  P_down(t)
  valve opening alpha(t)

states:
  m(t)
  U(t)

mass balance:
  dm/dt = m_dot_fw - m_dot_s

energy balance:
  dU/dt = Q_dot + m_dot_fw h_fw - m_dot_s h_g(P)

thermodynamic closure:
  solve P, m_l, m_v from:
    m = m_l + m_v
    U = m_l u_f(P) + m_v u_g(P)
    V = m_l v_f(P) + m_v v_g(P)

valve:
  m_dot_s = valve_flow(P, T_sat(P), P_down, alpha)
```

This model gives pressure as an output:

```text
P = P(m, U, V)
```

## 15. Numerical Simulation Algorithm

At every time step:

1. Start from current states `m` and `U`.
2. Solve the thermodynamic closure equations to find `P`, `m_l`, and `m_v`.
3. Compute saturation properties at `P`.
4. Compute valve area from opening `alpha`.
5. Compute steam outlet flow `m_dot_s`.
6. Evaluate:

```text
dm_dt = m_dot_fw - m_dot_s
dU_dt = Q_dot + m_dot_fw h_fw - m_dot_s h_g(P)
```

7. Integrate `m` and `U` forward using an ODE solver.
8. Repeat.

Recommended ODE solvers:

- explicit Runge-Kutta for slow transients,
- implicit methods for stiff systems,
- small time steps during fast valve opening or closing.

## 16. Thermodynamic Property Requirements

The model needs water and steam properties:

```text
T_sat(P)
rho_f(P), rho_g(P)
v_f(P), v_g(P)
u_f(P), u_g(P)
h_f(P), h_g(P)
```

Recommended sources:

- IAPWS-IF97 steam-water property formulation,
- CoolProp,
- XSteam,
- NIST REFPROP,
- validated plant steam tables.

Avoid mixing properties from different reference bases unless you are careful with internal energy and enthalpy offsets.

## 17. Important Physical Limits

The model should check:

```text
P > 0
m > 0
0 <= x <= 1 for saturated two-phase operation
V_l >= 0
V_v >= 0
m_dot_s >= 0
```

If `x <= 0`, the vessel has no vapor space and the pressure response becomes very stiff because liquid water is nearly incompressible.

If `x >= 1`, the vessel contains no liquid water and the steam may become superheated. Then the saturated two-phase model is no longer valid.

## 18. Interpretation of Pressure Dynamics

Pressure rises when the rate of energy accumulation is high relative to mass discharge:

```text
Q_dot + m_dot_fw h_fw > m_dot_s h_g(P) + energy absorbed by added mass
```

Pressure falls when valve discharge removes energy and mass faster than heat input and feedwater can replace it:

```text
m_dot_s h_g(P) > Q_dot + m_dot_fw h_fw
```

The pressure response is strongly nonlinear because:

- saturation temperature changes with pressure,
- latent heat changes with pressure,
- vapor density changes strongly with pressure,
- valve flow is nonlinear and may become choked,
- small vapor volumes cause faster pressure changes.

## 19. Practical Initial Conditions

To initialize the model, choose:

```text
P_0
water level or liquid volume V_l0
```

Then compute:

```text
V_v0 = V - V_l0
m_l0 = V_l0 / v_f(P_0)
m_v0 = V_v0 / v_g(P_0)
m_0 = m_l0 + m_v0
U_0 = m_l0 u_f(P_0) + m_v0 u_g(P_0)
```

These values are consistent with saturated equilibrium.


## 21. Common Simplifications

If only a rough pressure trend is required:

```text
m_dot_s = K_valve alpha sqrt(max(P - P_down, 0))
```

and:

```text
dP/dt approx K_p [Q_dot - m_dot_s h_g(P)]
```

where `K_p` is an empirical pressure-gain parameter. This can be useful for control design, but it is not a thermodynamically rigorous model.

For engineering simulation, the mass-energy model with steam-table closure is preferred.

## 22. Summary

The core of a steam drum pressure simulation is the coupling of:

```text
mass balance:
  dm/dt = inflow - outflow

energy balance:
  dU/dt = heat input + inlet enthalpy flow - outlet enthalpy flow

fixed-volume constraint:
  V = m_l v_f(P) + m_v v_g(P)

saturation thermodynamics:
  properties = functions of P

valve flow:
  m_dot_s = function(P, P_down, T, valve opening)
```

The most stable implementation is usually to integrate total mass `m` and internal energy `U`, then compute pressure from steam-water thermodynamic properties at each time step.
